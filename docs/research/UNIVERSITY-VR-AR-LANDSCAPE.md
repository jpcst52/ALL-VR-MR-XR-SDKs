# University VR/AR landscape: work we can build on or cite

A survey, made on 2026-11-01, of VR/AR research and programs at other universities and research groups that relate to the examples in this repository. It extends the annotated bibliography in [README.md](README.md) and does not repeat entries already there (Sacks et al. 2013, Scorgie et al. 2024, Poyade et al. 2021, Maresky et al. 2019, Moro et al. 2017, and the others listed in that file). New BibTeX entries are appended to [references.bib](references.bib) under the heading "University landscape (added 2026-11-01)".

## How this was checked

- Every paper below was matched to a bibliographic record that was actually retrieved in this session: a Crossref record (title, authors, venue, volume, pages, DOI), plus an abstract from Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, PubMed, arXiv, or a university repository page. The "verified at" column names the record used.
- **"Key finding (as stated)"** paraphrases the abstract or the record that was read. Nothing here comes from a full text. Where no abstract could be read, the row says "abstract not read" and no finding is given.
- **"Relevance"** is my inference, not a claim made by the source.
- Institutions come from the author affiliations in the OpenAlex record or the publisher/repository page. These are affiliations at publication time and may have changed.
- Program and lab descriptions come from the university page that was opened. A page that does not mention outcomes is not evidence of outcomes.
- Nothing here shows that any library, app, or approach works on the Quest, the Vive Pro 2, or in this repository's code. None of it has been tested here.

## Summary

1. **Safety training in VR generally does about as well as, or somewhat better than, conventional training, and the gains depend on the outcome measured.** A 2024 meta-analysis of construction safety training (Man et al.) reports pooled effects favouring VR for behaviors, skills and experience. A Finnish randomized trial with 119 construction workers (Nykänen et al., 2020) found stronger effects on motivation, self-efficacy and self-reported safety performance at one month. Neither measured injuries.
2. **The most useful design findings for us are specific ones.** In forklift VR (Hong Kong PolyU and University of South Australia group), scenario fidelity raised presence and usability but control-interface fidelity (wheel and pedals versus keyboard) did not, and higher presence did not strongly drive behavior. A virtual accident triggered by inattention reduced risk habituation for about a week (Texas A&M). In a Penn State battery-storage procedure study, VR beat video on spatial and procedural scores, but neither conveyed *why* the steps mattered.
3. **Process-industry (refinery, chemical) VR is still mostly system descriptions**: operator-training simulator reviews, a refinery augmented-VR system, and a refinery VR framework with a validation protocol that is planned, not reported. This confirms the gap noted in README.md section 2: outcome studies are rare.
4. **Electronics labs: AR results are mixed.** Saarland University's tablet AR (data shown beside the circuit) helped conceptual learning, while their head-mounted AR version did *worse* than a separate display. Immersive VR circuit work found here is mostly development papers without learning data.
5. **Anatomy and cardiovascular: meta-analyses point to small or uncertain gains.** Moro et al. (2021) found no significant pooled gain for VR/AR in physiology and anatomy. Zhao et al. (2020) found a moderate gain for VR in anatomy with very high heterogeneity, and Bölek et al. (2021) found no gain for AR. Heart-specific single studies (Zinchenko et al., 2020; Gonzalez et al., 2020 with AR) are positive but small and single-site.
6. **Collaborative and web-based VR in classes**: Stanford VHIL ran a large course largely in VR (Han et al., 2023) and reports effects of avatar type and environment on group behavior and presence. WebXR studies found are pilots measuring usability and presence, not learning.
7. **Measurement**: three instruments from the University of Copenhagen group (CAMIL model, Multimodal Presence Scale, and a VR-specific cognitive load scale) would strengthen the study designs. A systematic review of cybersickness with current headsets is also added.
8. **Open material**: Purdue has a CC BY-NC LOTO VR training package with a DOI. The OEXR library (EDUCAUSE XR Community Group, hosted at SDSU) is still "coming soon". NC State's XR site invites contributors. No openly licensed, evaluated industrial-safety VR courseware was found.

## Gaps and caveats

- **Abstract-level only.** No full text was read. Sample sizes, designs and effect sizes above are as the abstracts state them; read the full texts before citing any number.
- **Records without a publisher or index abstract.** Manca et al. (2013) and Ko et al. (2021): records confirmed, findings unknown. Makransky et al. (2017): summary from Labster's page (a company that sells VR labs), not from the publisher. Coban et al. (2022) and Patle et al. (2019): summaries from university repository pages.
- **Small discrepancies to confirm.** Bölek et al. (2021): Crossref lists three authors, OpenAlex four. Kim et al. (2023) iScience is dated 2022 online in OpenAlex and 2023 (vol. 26, issue 1) in Crossref. Moro et al. (2021) is 2020 online. Vercelli et al.'s affiliation is inferred.
- **Process-industry gap still holds.** No controlled learning-outcome study for refinery or chemical-plant VR training was found beyond Poyade et al. (2021, already in README.md). The refinery items found are system descriptions or a planned protocol.
- **Forklift evidence is about presence, usability, and situation awareness**, not accident reduction.
- **Electronics VR**: no controlled immersive-VR circuit learning study was verified; the AR evidence conflicts (tablet helped, headset did not).
- **Cardiovascular**: physiology-specific (cardiac cycle, circulation) VR evidence is thin; the heart studies found are mostly anatomy.
- **WebXR**: still only pilots measuring usability and presence.
- **Search limits.** About 30 web searches plus Crossref, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, PubMed, DataCite and arXiv lookups in one session. This is not a systematic search; Michigan and Georgia Tech programs were not found, and other relevant labs surely exist.
